## What you will learn

- Follow GitHub's five steps for communicating impact.
- Match each type of evidence to what it can and cannot support.

## Five steps and honest evidence

The [impact communication tutorial](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/roll-out-at-scale/communicate-copilot-impact) works in five steps. Define the decision the report supports. Establish the comparison. Assemble evidence: adoption, cost from billing tools (reporting credits separately from billed spend), code generation activity, delivery and quality measures, and feedback with respondent counts. Separate observations from conclusions, and do not convert lines of code or time saved into financial returns. Finally, write the narrative with recommendation options and define the next review with an owner.

The [company goals tutorial](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/roll-out-at-scale/drive-downstream-impact/achieve-company-goals) adds that usage signals are not outcomes. Choose one or two goals, record a baseline, align scope and period when comparing, check what else changed, measure quality alongside speed and investigate before attributing a change to Copilot. It notes that the impact dashboard compares cohorts rather than measuring before and after.

Guide advice: in the report, label each statement as an observation or a conclusion, and list what each piece of evidence does not establish.

## Worked example: the expansion report

**Hypothetical starting state:** Leadership asks whether to fund Copilot for the next year. The rollout owner has adoption data, billing data, 40 survey responses and pull request throughput figures.

The report opens with the decision: renew, renew with changes, or stop. The comparison is participating teams against their own baseline for the same period, noting a reorganization in one team. Evidence: adoption trend; actual spend against budget, with credits listed separately; survey themes with the respondent count; throughput changes labeled as associations. The conclusion recommends renewal with a quality check, and the next review is set for one quarter later with the rollout owner accountable.

**Outcome:** Leaders see what was observed and what is inferred. The figures are hypothetical.

## Practice: outline an outcome report

**Prerequisites:** Hypothetical or approved aggregate data and the two tutorials linked above. No settings need to be changed.

1. Write the decision and the options.
2. Define the comparison, including scope, period and other changes.
3. List each evidence type and what it does not establish.
4. Write two observations and one conclusion, labeled.
5. Name the next review date and owner.

**Expected result:** A one-page outline with labeled observations and conclusions.

<summary>Model answer</summary>

Decision: renew, change or stop. Comparison: same teams, same period length, reorganization noted. Adoption describes use, not value; billing shows spend, not savings; code generation shows activity, not productivity; delivery data shows association, not cause; feedback shows perception from a stated number of respondents. Next review in one quarter, owned by the rollout owner.
